# ADR 0006 — The Account and Identity Model

- **Status**: Accepted
- **Date**: 2026-07-30

## Context

ADR 0005 settled that linking Apple, Google, Steam and email identities to one account is a
Contour 2a capability: adopted, not written. It deliberately left *which* provider open, so
that the answer could come late.

That leaves a gap it did not notice. Adopting an authentication provider is reversible.
**The shape of the account is not**, because it is what the game's data hangs off — and we
have already started building on it in a way that will not survive:

```ts
private readonly arks = new Map<AccountId, Ark>();
```

One account owns one ark, forever. But a world has an `ends_at`: worlds are rounds, and a
person plays several of them over the years, having already been promised the ark's last
jump into the next world. Moving that map into the database unchanged makes the second
world a migration of everything that references an account.

Three further questions are cheap to answer today and expensive after the first live
account exists: what the login for a password identity is, whether two accounts can ever
become one, and what deletion means when battle reports point at the deleted party.

None of these depend on which provider is adopted. All of them constrain it.

## Decision

### 1. The account id is ours

`platform.accounts.id` is generated by us and is never a foreign system's identifier. Game
tables carry it as a foreign key.

Every hosted-authentication quickstart says the opposite — store the provider's `user_id`
on your rows and skip a table. Doing that here would put a vendor's identifier in the
foreign key of every holding, report and clan log we ever write, and make leaving that
vendor a rewrite of the references rather than a rewrite of the adapter.

Whatever is adopted therefore lands *beside* the account, never in place of it.

### 2. The account is world-independent, and it is not the player

Three levels, and the middle one is the one currently missing:

| | What it is | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|
| `platform.accounts` | a person | forever |
| a per-world row (`game.*`) | that person **in one world** | one round |
| `game.holdings` | what they own there | one round |

The account carries what survives a world ending: display name, purchases, platform state.
Everything scoped to a round hangs off the middle row, and game tables reference *it*, not
the account.

The middle row's definition belongs to #13 with the rest of the game schema. What this ADR
fixes is that it exists and that nothing round-scoped may attach to the account directly.

### 3. An account holds no credentials, and a display name is not one

Credentials live in `identities`, keyed by `(provider, external_id)`. That much CLAUDE.md
already required; what follows from it and was not written down is that **the display name
must be an account column**. An Apple identity is an opaque subject claim, and Google and
Steam give nothing meant to be read either — so a human-readable name cannot be derived
from an identity at all, no matter which provider is adopted.

It is globally unique, and it is not the login.

### 4. A password identity's `external_id` is an email address

Not a handle. A handle gives no recovery channel, and a forgotten password is then a
permanently lost account — including a paying one.

Nothing is shown to other players from it, because §3 put the visible name elsewhere.

### 5. Accounts are never merged

An account may gain and lose identities, always from inside an already-authenticated
session. Two accounts never become one.

Merging is not hard because of the identity tables; it is impossible because of the game.
Two accounts in the same world are two arks, two clan memberships and two purchase
histories, and there is no correct way to combine them — only a chosen loss, and a support
queue arguing about which.

### 6. Deletion anonymises the account and removes the identities

The account row survives as a tombstone, its display name and its identities do not. Game
history keeps its foreign keys and renders an unknown party.

A `DELETE` would either cascade into other players' battle reports or be refused by them.

## Alternatives Considered

- **`users` with a column per provider** — `google_id`, `apple_id`, `steam_id`. The
  idiomatic default and the shape most codebases have. Rejected: each new provider is a
  migration plus a partial unique index, and Steam arriving late is exactly the scenario
  this project expects.
- **The adopted provider's user id as the primary key everywhere**, which is what hosted
  auth documentation recommends and what makes their quickstart short. Rejected in §1. The
  cost is invisible until the day it is unpayable.
- **One account per world** — the account *is* the participation, no middle row. Genuinely
  simpler, and correct for a game that never resets. Rejected: purchases, display name,
  suspension and friendships all outlive a round, and the promised jump into the next world
  requires a person who outlives one too.
- **Supporting account merging**, which players will ask for the first week. Rejected in §5
  — and worth recording as rejected, because "we will just merge them" is easy to say
  before anyone looks at the game state.
- **A handle as the login, with an optional email**, which reads better for a game.
  Rejected: optional recovery is no recovery for whoever skipped it, and the population that
  skips it overlaps with the population that pays.
- **A per-world display name**, as Travian has. Not rejected — deferred. Adding a nullable
  override on the per-world row later is additive, so it fails the ADR bar rather than the
  design argument.

## Consequences

- **"What does this player own" is two joins**, account → per-world row → holdings, in a
  query that would otherwise be one. Paid on every read for the whole life of the project.
- **We will run two account tables.** An adopted auth library brings its own `user` row,
  and §1 keeps ours, joined by a mapping. Duplication accepted knowingly in exchange for
  the foreign key being ours; anyone who finds it later will think it is an oversight.
- **A second, empty account is a real failure mode.** Somebody who signed up with Apple and
  later taps "continue with Google" gets a new account and an empty world, and §5 means
  support cannot repair it. The mitigation is client-side — remember the last provider used,
  make linking prominent inside the session — and mitigation is all it is.
- **A global display name namespace exhausts** over years of rounds, and nobody can start a
  round anonymously. Renaming is safe to sell, since it cannot affect an outcome.
- **Losing access to an email loses the account**, unless another identity was linked first.
- **Deletion is not `DELETE`**, so every query touching accounts must respect a tombstone,
  and "delete my data" needs an explicit story rather than a cascade.

## Open Questions

- **Which authentication provider is adopted.** Still 0005's question; this ADR exists so
  that answering it touches `platform` and nothing else.
- **A contact email on the account**, separate from the password login — Apple relay
  addresses and payment receipts both point that way, and neither exists yet.
- **Whether one person may hold two worlds at once.** Left open deliberately: the schema is
  identical either way, so it is a policy decision and spending an ADR on it would be
  spending it on nothing.
- **Session and token format.** Unaffected by everything above, because a use case receives
  a `Principal` and never a token.
